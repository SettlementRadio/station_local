# 65-arts.md — story, image & cultural memory

> **Cornerstone file — how the worlds imagine.** Music has its own domain in `70-music.md`; the
> remembered artists are in `15-figures.md`. This file covers story, visual work, performance,
> criticism and the rooms in which art becomes public. See `README.md` for the authoring
> conventions.

## Stories that can travel

Story holds first place among the settled arts because it survives bad conditions. A sculpture
needs mass allowance, a painting needs a safe surface, and a performance needs bodies in the same
room. A story can cross by voice, text or memory. It can be copied at a relay, carried in a ship's
small library, retold in a canteen and returned to its maker with an ending they never wrote. This
does not make other arts less valued. It makes story the common form through which people discuss
nearly all of them.

### The Exodus {#the-exodus}

The Exodus is the departure story: humanity leaving the Old System in many migrations and later
memory compressing them into one great outward movement. Plays usually place the argument before
departure, while novels linger on what the travellers misunderstood about arrival. Every settled
world can tell the story without agreeing on who was brave, who was abandoned or whether leaving
was a promise kept.

### First Contact That Never Was {#first-contact-that-never-was}

First Contact That Never Was is the shared story of expectation meeting silence. It appears in
children's tales as a visitor who does not arrive, in comedies as a reception committee with nobody
to receive and in serious work as an argument about obligations to minds humanity may never meet.
The name is wry rather than despairing. The worlds keep listening and keep making room in their
stories.

### The Silence {#the-silence}

The Silence is told from the room after the signal stops: settlements unable to know whether their
neighbours still live, families preserving messages with no address that can answer, and local
histories growing apart. Art rarely treats it as empty time. It is the period in which isolated
worlds made their own answers, many of which survived the Reconnection.

### Hard, Soft and Social Speculation {#speculation-schools}

Speculative writers keep an old three-way argument. Hard Speculation accepts only premises its
makers can defend from known science. Soft Speculation allows an uncertain premise when the human
consequence is exact. Social Speculation changes an institution rather than a machine and follows
who gains a room, a vote, a meal or a way out.

### Contact Speculation {#contact-speculation}

Contact Speculation imagines other minds without claiming the Search for Others has found them. Its
strongest works leave something unresolved: a signal that could be natural, a visitor whose purpose
cannot be translated, or a human interpretation exposed as vanity. The genre is less interested in
winning an encounter than in what duties begin before certainty does.

### Responsive Literature {#responsive-literature}

Responsive Literature makes the relay lag part of the form. A work is sent with spaces for distant
answers, and receiving libraries bind local replies behind it before sending the enlarged copy on.
The argument may circle back after the original fashion has passed. Writers therefore learn to be
answered by readers they will never meet and whose version of the question may be better.

## The maker, the material and the room

Physical art is local even when its reputation is not. Gravity decides how a body enters a stage,
how a sculpture bears its weight and whether an audience stands on a floor or clips to a rail.
Atmosphere changes sound, drying time and what can safely be released into a public room. Scarcity
decides whether a maker receives new material, clean scrap or the back of a panel already painted
on its other side. These conditions are part of the work's account of where it was made.

### The Synthesis School {#synthesis-school}

The Synthesis School begins with a human design and gives repeated or exacting execution to machine
tools. Its supporters say authorship lies in the choice, the limit and the refusal, not in making
every mark by hand. Its critics ask how much labour can be handed away before a work becomes a
specification. The machines are treated as tools throughout; the dispute is between people about
credit and work.

### The Material Tradition {#material-tradition}

The Material Tradition leaves evidence of making visible: a repaired edge, a changed batch of
pigment, a tool mark or the join where local stock ran out. Frontier workshops favour it because
perfect replacement material is rarely available, but it is not a claim that hand tools are morally
pure. A maker may use complex equipment and still belong to the tradition if the finished work is
honest about what the equipment and the material did.

### Immersive Theatre and Circle Theatre {#theatre-forms}

Immersive Theatre grew in large core venues able to move light, sound, walls and audiences through
several spaces. Circle Theatre grew where one cleared room and a ring of chairs had to be enough.
Neither name guarantees quality. Immersive productions are mocked when machinery does the acting;
Circle productions are mocked when closeness is used to excuse poor rehearsal. Both forms travel,
but each becomes different when installed under another world's gravity and fire rules.

### The Weight Ledger {#weight-ledger}

A travelling physical work carries a Weight Ledger. It records the gravity and support for which
the work was made, the material substitutions already accepted, the safe handling points and the
changes made at each installation. If the work cannot be mounted elsewhere without becoming a
different object, the ledger says so plainly. Galleries then exhibit a study, a material sample or
the ledger itself rather than pretending the original has travelled.

### The Clear Hand Rule {#clear-hand-rule}

Station art obeys the Clear Hand Rule: no work may hide a handhold, inspection panel, route mark or
emergency light. Makers often build around these interruptions, and maintenance crews sign the
installation record beside the curator. The rule has spared many fine arguments and at least as
many bruised knuckles. A beautiful corridor still has to work in the dark.

### The Witness Patch {#witness-patch}

When a station passage is repainted or given a new mural, a small patch of the previous surface is
usually left beside a service panel. The custom is the wall-bound cousin of the witness seams in
`56-style.md`: not every old layer is preserved, but its prior use is not erased without notice.
Children compare patches while adults argue that the best one was covered long ago.

## Copies, drift and local hands

Art crosses the worlds at two speeds. A description, script or set of fabrication instructions can
travel by signal. The object, the cast and the maker travel by ship, if they travel at all. People
often know the argument around a work before they can stand beside it. By physical arrival the work
has already been imagined badly, defended passionately and imitated from incomplete accounts.

### The drift {#the-drift}

The drift is the cultural time-lag between scenes. A form may be abandoned in the core while a
frontier workshop is only beginning its own version; a frontier practice may be praised as a new
discovery by critics who never knew it continued.

### The Frontier Aesthetic {#frontier-aesthetic}

The Frontier Aesthetic grew inside the drift: direct materials, visible joins and little patience
for finish that cannot survive repair. Core imitations are resented when they copy the surface
while enjoying conditions that remove the need.

### Relay Editions {#relay-editions}

A Relay Edition is a physical work transmitted as measurements, instructions and tolerances for a
local workshop to realise. The originating maker and the receiving fabricators share the credit,
and every substitution is named. Exact copies are neither expected nor especially admired. What
matters is that another set of hands answered the same proposal with the gravity, tools and stock
available under another sky.

### The Second Opening {#second-opening}

A much-discussed work may have a signal opening and a later physical opening. The first is the
broadcast, script or review that reaches a settlement; the second comes when the freight arrives or
a Relay Edition is built. At the second opening, visitors compare the actual room with the work
they had assembled in their heads. The most common verdict is that the imagined version was larger.

## Works everyone argues over

### The Latecomers {#the-latecomers}

*The Latecomers* remains the most widely read novel in the settled worlds. Its anonymous author
tells of a generation ship that learns its destination was settled by people from nearer shores
while it was still crossing, and continues toward a world that no longer needs its mission. Schools
argue over whether the book is about duty, pride, migration or the right to change one's mind. The
Anonymous remains unnamed in `15-figures.md`; attribution games are part of the culture, not an
invitation for canon to settle them.

### The Silence Chronicles {#the-silence-chronicles}

*The Silence Chronicles* is a cycle of plays about worlds that never reconnect. Its casts are large
because each settlement develops a separate account of what humanity became, and staging the whole
cycle strains even core theatres. Small companies perform one room or one correspondence at a time.
Arguments over those cuts have become almost as durable as arguments over the plays.

### The Empty Chair {#the-empty-chair}

*The Empty Chair* exists in locally made versions rather than as one object sent everywhere. Each
shows an unoccupied chair facing an opening, but the material, scale and meaning belong partly to
the receiving world. A station version may be fixed to the deck; a heavy-world version may be cut
low and broad. Their ledgers name the workshops and repairs, and no authorised version is called a
replica.

### The Station Canon {#station-canon}

The Station Canon is the unofficial group of works Settlement Radio treats as common references.
It has no published boundary. Listeners infer it from repeats, adaptations and the works presenters
assume need no introduction, then complain that the core is overrepresented or that a local work
has been mistaken for a universal one.

## Radio in the gallery

Settlement Radio cannot reproduce a visual work and does not claim to. Its arts programmes make
something else: an account of the person who made the work, the material decisions, the reason it
occupies this room and the disagreement among those standing there. Room tone, footsteps, tools and
audience voices give the listener evidence without turning the presenter into a substitute pair of
eyes.

### The Room Rule {#room-rule}

The Room Rule governs visual-art reviews on the station. A review must identify the maker, the
material constraint, the argument around the work and what the room asks a visitor to do. It may
give enough physical description to orient the listener, but it must never pretend that description
is the work. A presenter who spends the whole item naming colours is expected to start again.

### The Listening Bench {#listening-bench}

Many galleries keep a Listening Bench near the exit, with a plain recorder and no questions on the
wall. Visitors may leave an account of what held them, irritated them or made no sense. Curators
send selections with the review copy, including disagreement and silence long enough to be awkward.
These voices let a radio audience hear the room change around a work without being told what to
think.

### The Handling Table {#handling-table}

A Handling Table holds approved offcuts, failed trials and material samples from an exhibition.
Visitors who cannot see the work, children told not to touch anything and makers curious about the
join all meet there. The samples are labelled as evidence, not miniatures. On radio, their weight,
sound and repair marks are often more useful than a long account of appearance.

The station's curators travel slowly and leave local makers to speak for what they cannot carry. A
programme may reach a world before the work, after it, or without it ever arriving.

## Canon facts

1. **Story is the most portable settled art: it can cross by voice, text or memory when a physical
   work, cast or instrument cannot travel.**
2. **Art moves at two speeds, with descriptions and instructions travelling by signal while people
   and objects travel by ship, so local scenes often answer work they have not physically received.**
3. **Settlement Radio keeps cultural memory by carrying criticism, performances and makers' voices
   between worlds; it does not claim that a broadcast replaces being in the room.**
4. **The Exodus, First Contact That Never Was and the Silence are the three shared story families,
   retold differently because the worlds disagree about departure, solitude and reconnection.**
5. **The Synthesis School gives exacting execution to machine tools, while the Material Tradition
   leaves repairs, substitutions and tool marks visible; their dispute is about human labour and
   credit, not machine personhood.**
6. **Hard Speculation limits itself to defensible science, Soft Speculation permits an uncertain
   premise for an exact human consequence, and Social Speculation changes an institution and
   follows who is affected.**
7. ***The Latecomers* is the most widely read settled novel, an anonymous work about a generation
   ship continuing toward a destination that was settled before it arrived.**
8. ***The Silence Chronicles* is a large cycle of plays about worlds that never reconnect; smaller
   companies commonly stage one room or correspondence rather than the whole work.**
9. ***The Empty Chair* exists as authorised local versions in different materials and gravities;
   their ledgers credit the workshops and repairs, and none is treated as a mere replica.**
10. **The drift is the cultural lag between scenes; the Frontier Aesthetic grew from direct
    materials and visible repair, and core copies are resented when they imitate its surface
    without its conditions.**
11. **The Station Canon is the unofficial group of works the station treats as common references;
    listeners infer its boundary from programming and regularly dispute whose work it leaves out.**
12. **Contact Speculation imagines other minds without treating rumour as evidence, and its strongest
    works leave some part of the encounter unresolved.**
13. **Immersive Theatre uses large venues to move an audience through several spaces; Circle Theatre
    uses one cleared room and a ring of chairs, and both forms change under local gravity and safety
    rules.**
14. **Responsive Literature travels with spaces for distant replies, which receiving libraries bind
    behind the work before sending the enlarged copy onward.**
15. **A Weight Ledger travels with a physical artwork and records its intended gravity, support,
    handling points, accepted substitutions and changes made at each installation.**
16. **A Relay Edition sends measurements, instructions and tolerances to a receiving workshop;
    originating maker and local fabricators share credit, and every material substitution is named.**
17. **A Witness Patch leaves a small part of an older station wall or mural visible after repainting,
    extending the witness-seam custom from clothing to shared architecture.**
18. **The Clear Hand Rule bars station art from hiding handholds, inspection panels, route marks or
    emergency lights, and maintenance crews sign the installation record with the curator.**
19. **The Room Rule requires a station review to cover the maker, material constraint, dispute and
    visitor's place in the room, without pretending that spoken description reproduces visual art.**
20. **A Listening Bench lets gallery visitors record what held, irritated or confused them; curators
    send selected reactions and disagreements with the station's review material.**
21. **A Handling Table offers labelled offcuts, failed trials and material samples that visitors may
    touch; the pieces are evidence of making, not miniature versions of the work.**
22. **A widely discussed work may have a signal opening and a later physical opening, when visitors
    compare the arrived or locally built work with the version they imagined from the broadcast.**
