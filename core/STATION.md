# STATION.md — the station's identity and the world's premise

## The station

Settlement Radio is the continuous speech-led news and talk service of the settled worlds. It
carries reporting, conversation, interviews, analysis and documentary work. Music is part of the
service, not its default format. Programming is set by the station rather than chosen by individual
presenters.

Humanity lives across scattered worlds, moons and stations, six centuries on from the present.
Travel between neighbouring worlds takes weeks. Radio is the thread that connects them: a wide
broadcast is repeated through the relay network and reaches the settled region in hours, while
people and physical goods remain separated by travel time.

The station broadcasts from a drifting facility among the main relay routes. It holds no permanent
world, serves no single polity and depends on infrastructure maintained across the settlements. Its
position and shared use make neutrality a practical condition of its existence.

## Standing facts

1. Settlement Radio is a continuously broadcasting news and talk station for the settled worlds;
   its central work is speech, and music is one part of the service.
2. Earth is the distant origin of settled humanity, spoken of fondly but holding no ancestral
   authority over the worlds that grew beyond it.
3. Travel between neighbouring worlds takes weeks. There is no faster-than-light travel or
   communication, and distance remains a normal condition of work, government, family and reporting.
4. The station's wide signal travels relay to relay in hours, allowing scattered listeners to hear
   the same broadcast within the lag rather than at one simultaneous instant.
5. Addressed messages wait in relay queues and can take days, weeks or months according to distance;
   a reply may arrive after the circumstances described in the first message have changed.
6. Settlement Radio occupies a drifting position among the relay routes, belongs to no world and
   remains neutral because every region depends on the same shared channel.
7. The word *settlement* is a linguistic fossil. Many worlds are old, urban and planet-spanning,
   but the early name endured, and the station's name carries that history without implying that
   its listeners still live in new colonies.
8. The station's tone is cozy, intelligent and a little wry, with optimism tempered by difficult
   questions. It is neither dystopian nor camp, and warmth does not replace plain factual speech.
